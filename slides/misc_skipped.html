Affine Cipher (explicit encode/decode)

Alphabet. Map letters \(A\!\to\!0,\ B\!\to\!1,\ \dots,\ Z\!\to\!25\) (ignore spaces/punctuation).

Key. Choose \(a=9,\ b=5,\ m=26\). Since \(\gcd(9,26)=1\), \(a^{-1}\equiv 3\pmod{26}\).

Encrypt. \(E(x)\equiv (9x+5)\bmod 26\)
Decrypt. \(D(y)\equiv a^{-1}(y-b)\equiv 3(y-5)\bmod 26\)

Example (plaintext): DATA SCIENCE

  • Numbers: \(D\,A\,T\,A\,S\,C\,I\,E\,N\,C\,E\ \to\ 3,0,19,0,18,2,8,4,13,2,4\)
  • Encrypt via \(9x+5\) (mod \(26\)): \[ 3\!\to\!6,\ 0\!\to\!5,\ 19\!\to\!20,\ 0\!\to\!5,\ 18\!\to\!11,\ 2\!\to\!23,\ 8\!\to\!25,\ 4\!\to\!15,\ 13\!\to\!18,\ 2\!\to\!23,\ 4\!\to\!15 \]
  • Back to letters: \(6,5,20,5,11,23,25,15,18,23,15\ \to\ \texttt{GFUF LXZPSXP}\)

Quick decode check. \(G(6)\ \mapsto\ D(3)\) because \(D(6)=3(6-5)\equiv 3\pmod{26}\);
\(X(23)\ \mapsto\ C(2)\) since \(D(23)=3(23-5)=54\equiv 2\pmod{26}\).

Data-science tie: solving \(ax\equiv b\pmod{26}\) is the same modular-inverse algebra behind simple substitution ciphers and modular encodings.

CRT as “Two Dials”

Picture. Two clock faces: - Dial A: mod \(m_1\) (ticks \(0,\dots,m_1-1\)) - Dial B: mod \(m_2\) (ticks \(0,\dots,m_2-1\))

Advance one tick of absolute time \(\Rightarrow\) both hands advance by \(+1\) (wrapping around). Solving \(x\equiv a\pmod{m_1},\ x\equiv b\pmod{m_2}\) means: > Find the absolute tick \(x\) where Dial A reads \(a\) and Dial B reads \(b\) simultaneously.

Why it works: If \(\gcd(m_1,m_2)=1\), the pair of readings cycles through every \((r_1,r_2)\) exactly once in \(0,\dots,m_1m_2-1\).

Two Dials — Worked Example

Puzzle. Find \(x\) with \(x\equiv 2\pmod 3\) and \(x\equiv 1\pmod 5\).

Dial states (absolute tick \(\to\) readings):

\(0\!\to\!(0,0),\ 1\!\to\!(1,1),\ 2\!\to\!(2,2),\ 3\!\to\!(0,3),\ 4\!\to\!(1,4),\ 5\!\to\!(2,0),\ 6\!\to\!(0,1),\ 7\!\to\!(1,2),\ 8\!\to\!(2,3),\ 9\!\to\!(0,4),\ 10\!\to\!(1,0),\ 11\!\to\!(2,1),\ 12\!\to\!(0,2),\ 13\!\to\!(1,3),\ 14\!\to\!(2,4)\)

We need \((2,1)\) \(\Rightarrow\) occurs at tick \(11\).
Answer: \(x\equiv 11\pmod{15}\).

Two Dials — Algebra Behind the Picture

Given \(x\equiv a\pmod{m_1},\ x\equiv b\pmod{m_2}\) with \(\gcd(m_1,m_2)=1\):

  1. Write \(x=a+m_1t\).
  2. Plug in: \(a+m_1t\equiv b\pmod{m_2}\ \Rightarrow\ m_1 t\equiv b-a\pmod{m_2}\).
  3. Since \(\gcd(m_1,m_2)=1\), \(m_1\) has an inverse \((m_1)^{-1}\pmod{m_2}\), so
    \(t\equiv (b-a)\,(m_1)^{-1}\pmod{m_2}\).
  4. Substitute back: \(x=a+m_1 t\) and reduce \(\bmod\ m_1m_2\).

Example recap (3 & 5).
\(x=2+3t\), and \(3t\equiv 1-2\equiv -1\equiv 4\ (\bmod 5)\).
\(3^{-1}\equiv 2\ (\bmod 5)\), so \(t\equiv 4\cdot2\equiv 8\equiv 3\ (\bmod 5)\).
Thus \(x=2+3\cdot 3=11\ (\bmod 15)\).
