🎪 Sages & Tricksters


Setup

  • Draw one card from the envelope. Keep it hidden.
  • Black = Sage. Always tell the truth.
  • Red = Trickster. Always lie.
  • The six cards are printed on the envelope.
  • The fact on the slip is true.

Goal

Find the tricksters.

🔄 How to play

1. Discovery — two rounds

  • Each round, every player asks one person a yes/no question about their card.
  • Sages answer truthfully. Tricksters lie.

2. Debate

  • Compare answers. Look for contradictions.
  • You may not say which card you hold, or which role you are.

3. Conclusion

  • Agree on everyone’s roles, then reveal.
🧿 Asking well
  • Ask about a card’s suit or rank.
  • Asking about a card’s colour is allowed. Try it and see what happens.

Click “Asking well” bottom right when you are ready, or leave it shut.

“Is your card red?” gets no from everyone; “is your card black?” gets yes from everyone. Say only that colour questions are a trap, not why — let them predict it. Hold the real method back: if a group stalls, hint that two properties which cannot both be true of one card will do it.

What did you just do?

Write down the step that convinced you:


“I knew __________ was a Trickster because __________.”

Collect two or three. Nearly all take the form: if they were a Sage, then X and not-X. Do not name it yet.

Lying is an operator

Let \(1\) mean yes and \(0\) mean no.

They say 1.

Two rows fit. Nothing so far separates them.

To eliminate one, you must fix one of the input columns.

You cannot fix they lie — that is the unknown.

So fix their card has it.


What could you ask that would do that?

Drive the discussion from the table. Walk down the right column one reveal at a time and let them supply the last step.

The answer they should reach: ask about a property whose value you already know for that person — then the “has it” column is fixed, only two rows remain, and those two differ in the output. So the answer they give reports the lie bit directly.

Either value works. Fix the column to 0 and the top two rows survive, so a “yes” means lying. Fix it to 1 and the bottom two survive, so a “no” means lying. If a group offers one direction, ask for the other.

In practice known-false is much easier to find. After a “yes” the surviving cards are (black with the property) together with (red without it) — a set spanning both colours. Naming a suit or rank none of them has is easy; finding something a mixed bag all share is rare.

Careful with the “true of at least one card” rule. It bans a question that is false of every card in the packet. It does not ban one that is false of every card this person could still be holding, which is what the strategy actually needs — and that is usually still legal. Packet 9, after a yes to “spade”: club, heart and even-number all work, and club is legal because 2C is in the packet even though no candidate is a club.

The clean two-question kill is available after 192 of 219 possible first answers, about 88%. In the remaining cases no single follow-up fixes the column, and the group has to combine what they heard across several people instead – which is what the debate round is for.

Do not name XOR. The Truth slide introduces the same four-row shape for OR, and naming it there lands better.

Later in the term, the direction is the point: a truth table is normally read left to right, inputs to output. All hour they read it right to left. That is what you do with data — you see what came out and argue about what went in.

Both are inference. The method follows from the information.

The discriminator is whether the evidence reaches as far as the claim does. Exact inference is not a different mental operation; it is what inference becomes when the domain is small enough to exhaust.

This course is the left-hand column. Most of their degree is the right. Do not oversell it yet — lecture 3 earns it, when the same cereal claim is asked first of a dataset and then of every cereal ever manufactured.

Summary

\(p\) \(q\) \(p \lor q\)
F F F
F T T
T F T
T T T
  • The diagram is a Truth Table
  • \(p\), \(q\) are variables taking one of two Boolean values (T/F, 1/0)
  • \(\lor\) is a binary operator: a function from 2 Boolean inputs to 1 Boolean output, characterized by its output column
  • \(\lor\) corresponds to the English or
  • \(p \lor q\) is a proposition

Cool down

  • How many rows for a proposition on 3 variables? On \(k\)?


  • Name an operator taking 1 variable: __________


  • Name an operator taking 3 variables: __________


  • How many different binary operators could there be?

\(2^k\) rows. \(2^{2^k}\) operators on \(k\) variables; 16 binary ones. Half the binary operators are commutative (8).
